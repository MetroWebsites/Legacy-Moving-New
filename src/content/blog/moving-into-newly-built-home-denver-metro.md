## TL;DR

Moving into a newly built home in the Denver metro area requires special planning beyond a typical move. Coordinate your move-in date with construction completion, allowing buffer time for inevitable delays. Conduct thorough walk-throughs to identify issues before closing. Understand your builder's warranty and document everything. Set up utilities early, as new addresses sometimes cause delays. Protect new floors, walls, and fixtures during move-in. Be prepared for landscaping, window treatments, and finishing touches that builders don't include. Research HOA rules and community requirements. Plan for construction debris cleanup and potential neighborhood inconveniences. Work with movers experienced in new construction move-ins who understand the importance of protecting pristine surfaces and can navigate construction zones safely.

## Understanding New Construction Timelines

One of the biggest challenges with new construction is the unpredictable timeline:

**Expect Delays**: Construction delays are the norm, not the exception. Weather, supply chain issues, labor shortages, inspection delays, and countless other factors can push your completion date. In Colorado, winter weather can particularly impact construction schedules. Never assume your home will be ready exactly when the builder initially projects.

**Buffer Your Move Date**: When planning your move, build in a substantial buffer. If your builder says your home will be ready March 1st, don't schedule your move for March 2nd. Plan for at least 2-4 weeks after the projected completion date, especially if moving from a rental property. Consider overlap time where you maintain your current residence while the new home is finalized.

**Rental Flexibility**: If you're currently renting, negotiate a month-to-month lease extension or flexible end date if possible. Explain your situation to your landlord—most understand construction uncertainty. Having flexibility prevents the nightmare scenario of being homeless if your new home isn't ready.

**Storage Solutions**: If your current home situation has a firm end date before your new home is guaranteed ready, arrange for [short-term storage](/services/storage). This allows you to move out on schedule even if you can't move into your new home immediately.

**Closing Date vs. Move-In Date**: Your closing date and move-in date should not be the same day. Close on the home, then schedule your physical move for a few days later. This gives you time for final walk-throughs, key pickup, cleaning, and any last-minute issues without the pressure of movers waiting.

**Weekly Updates**: Stay in close contact with your builder, especially in the final weeks. Request weekly (or even more frequent) updates. Visit the construction site regularly if possible to monitor progress yourself. Take photos on each visit to document the state of construction and any concerns.

**Occupancy Permit**: Confirm your home has received its Certificate of Occupancy (CO) from the local municipality before moving in. Some builders will try to delay move-ins if the CO hasn't been issued. Most cities around Denver require this before you can legally occupy the home. Your builder should provide a copy at closing.

## Pre-Closing Walk-Throughs

Thorough walk-throughs before closing are essential:

**Multiple Inspections**: Don't rely on a single walk-through. Visit your home at different construction stages:
- **Frame Stage**: See the structure before drywall goes up, checking framing, plumbing rough-in, electrical rough-in, and HVAC installation
- **Pre-Drywall**: Final chance to see behind walls, verify outlet and switch placement, confirm proper insulation
- **Pre-Closing Walk-Through**: Complete inspection of finished home, typically 5-7 days before closing

**The Final Walk-Through Checklist**: On your final walk-through (ideally a few days before closing), systematically inspect everything:

**Exterior**:
- Check foundation for cracks or settling issues
- Inspect siding, brick, or stone for damage or installation issues
- Test all exterior doors and locks
- Verify garage door operation and safety sensors
- Check driveway, walkways, and any completed landscaping
- Inspect roof and gutters (use binoculars if necessary)
- Test exterior outlets, light fixtures, and any outdoor electrical
- Check grading to ensure water drains away from the house

**Interior - Each Room**:
- Test every light switch and outlet with a plug tester
- Open and close every window, checking for smooth operation and proper sealing
- Open and close every door, ensuring proper fit and operation
- Check flooring for damage, uneven areas, or installation issues
- Inspect paint for quality, coverage, and touch-ups needed
- Look for drywall issues—cracks, uneven areas, poor finishing
- Check trim and molding for gaps, poor fitting, or damage
- Verify all requested outlets (USB outlets, etc.) are installed correctly

**Kitchen**:
- Test all appliances (bring a pan of water to boil on stove, run dishwasher through full cycle, etc.)
- Run water in sink, checking for leaks and proper drainage
- Test garbage disposal
- Open and close every cabinet and drawer multiple times
- Check countertops for proper installation, seams, and any damage
- Verify backsplash installation quality
- Test under-cabinet lighting if installed

**Bathrooms**:
- Run all faucets and showers, checking water pressure and temperature
- Fill tubs and sinks, check drainage and look for leaks underneath
- Flush toilets repeatedly, ensuring proper operation
- Test all exhaust fans
- Check tile work for proper installation, grout quality, and any cracks
- Verify caulking around tubs, showers, and sinks
- Test any built-in features like medicine cabinets or towel warmers

**HVAC**:
- Run heating and cooling systems
- Check airflow from all vents
- Listen for unusual noises
- Verify thermostat operation
- Confirm all rooms heat and cool properly
- Check that return air vents are installed and open

**Plumbing**:
- Check water heater operation (temperature, pilot light if gas)
- Look under sinks for any leaks or improper connections
- Test water pressure throughout house
- Verify hot water reaches all fixtures promptly
- Check washing machine connections
- Inspect sump pump operation if applicable

**Electrical**:
- Test the main electrical panel—all breakers should be labeled
- Verify all ceiling fans operate on all speeds
- Check door bell operation
- Test smoke detectors and carbon monoxide detectors
- Verify any specialty electrical (security system prewiring, network cables, etc.)

**Document Everything**: Bring a clipboard, notepad, and camera on your walk-through. Create a punch-list of every item needing attention. Take photos of problems. Many builders provide forms for punch-list items, but maintain your own detailed list as backup. Be thorough—this is your last opportunity to identify issues before closing when the builder is obligated to fix them.

**Bring Tools**: Consider bringing:
- Outlet tester (home improvement stores sell these for $5-10)
- Flashlight for checking dark areas
- Tape measure for verifying room dimensions if questioned
- Ladder for checking hard-to-reach areas
- Level for checking floor/counter levelness if concerned

**Professional Inspection**: Even with new construction, consider hiring a professional home inspector for an unbiased evaluation. This costs $300-500 typically but can identify problems you might miss. Some issues may not be apparent to untrained eyes. Denver has many inspectors familiar with new construction specifics.

**Builder Presence**: Request that your builder representative or site superintendent attend the walk-through to address questions immediately and acknowledge issues. Get written confirmation that identified problems will be fixed before or shortly after closing.

## Understanding Builder Warranties

New construction comes with warranty protection, but understanding the details is crucial:

**Types of Coverage**: Most builders provide:
- **1-Year Workmanship Warranty**: Covers construction defects, material defects, and installation issues
- **2-Year Systems Warranty**: Covers major systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC
- **10-Year Structural Warranty**: Covers major structural defects in foundation, framing, and structural elements
- Many Denver builders also provide third-party warranty backing through companies like 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty or similar

**What's Typically Covered**:
- Structural defects (foundation, framing, load-bearing elements)
- Major systems failures (plumbing, electrical, HVAC systems)
- Roofing defects and leaks
- Workmanship issues (poor drywall, flooring issues, etc.)
- Manufacturing defects in materials

**What's Typically NOT Covered**:
- Normal wear and tear
- Homeowner neglect or failure to maintain
- Damage from alterations made by homeowner
- Landscaping and irrigation (usually 30-90 day warranty only)
- Appliances (covered by manufacturer warranties instead)
- Minor cosmetic issues after acceptance
- Issues resulting from severe weather or acts of God

**Documentation Requirements**: Maintain all warranty documentation in a safe place. Create a home binder with:
- All builder warranty information
- Appliance manuals and warranties
- Contact information for builder's warranty department
- Photos of your home at move-in
- Records of all warranty claims and service

**Reporting Issues**: Most warranties require timely reporting of issues. Don't wait months to report problems. Your builder will provide a warranty service request process—follow it carefully and keep records of all communications.

**30-Day Follow-Up**: Many builders schedule a 30-day follow-up inspection where they address minor issues that have surfaced after move-in. Document anything you notice in your first month.

**11-Month Walk-Through**: Near the end of your first year, conduct another thorough inspection and document any issues to report before your workmanship warranty expires. This is particularly important in Denver where seasonal changes (winter settling, summer expansion) might reveal issues not apparent initially.

**Third-Party Warranty**: If your builder provides third-party warranty backing, understand that process. If the builder goes out of business or is unresponsive, the third-party warranty company becomes your recourse.

**Maintenance Requirements**: Some warranties require proof of regular maintenance. Keep records of HVAC filter changes, recommended system services, and other maintenance tasks.

## Setting Up Utilities and Services

New construction addresses sometimes complicate utility setup:

**Start Early**: Begin utility setup 2-3 weeks before your planned move-in date. New addresses sometimes aren't in utility company systems immediately, causing delays.

**Confirm the Address**: Verify your official street address with your builder and the local municipality. Sometimes the planned address changes during development. Ensure you're setting up utilities at the correct address.

**Electric Service (Xcel Energy)**: Most of the Denver metro area is served by Xcel Energy. For new construction:
- The builder typically has electric service in the construction name during building
- You'll need to transfer service to your name starting from your move-in date
- For some new developments, permanent electric service may not be fully activated until close to completion
- Have your closing documents ready as proof of ownership when setting up service

**Gas Service (Xcel Energy or Local Providers)**: Same process as electric. Some Denver suburbs have different gas providers. The builder can tell you which company serves your development.

**Water and Sewer**: Services vary by location:
- Denver Water serves the city and some suburbs
- Many suburban areas have their own water districts
- Contact your specific water provider to initiate service
- You may need your closing paperwork as proof of ownership
- Some municipalities require a water service deposit

**Internet and Cable**: Major providers in the Denver metro area include Xcel/Comcast, CenturyLink, and Ting in some areas. New developments might have:
- Limited provider options (some have exclusive agreements with one provider)
- Service not yet available (infrastructure still being installed in very new developments)
- Pre-wiring for specific providers
Contact providers well in advance to confirm availability and schedule installation. Installation appointments can book up weeks in advance, especially during peak moving season.

**Trash and Recycling**: Service depends on your location:
- Denver city residents use Denver solid waste services
- Many suburban cities contract with specific providers
- Some HOAs include trash service in fees
- Others allow homeowner choice of provider
Check with your builder or HOA for requirements and procedures.

**Security Systems**: If your home is prewired for security:
- Ask builder which company provided prewiring and if there are any restrictions
- Get the wiring documentation showing where sensors/panels are located
- Shop systems before moving in, but schedule installation after move-in to avoid movers damaging sensors

**Smart Home Setup**: Many new Denver homes include smart home features:
- Get information on all installed systems from your builder
- Set up accounts for systems before moving day
- Consider having a tech-savvy friend help with initial setup
- Test all smart features during your walk-through

## Protecting Your New Home During Move-In

Your brand-new home needs extra protection during the move:

**Floor Protection**: This is critical in new construction:
- **Hardwood Floors**: Use ram board (protective floor covering) or clean moving blankets on all hardwood floors. Even careful movers can scuff or scratch new floors with heavy items. Don't rely on the thin padding often used—insist on substantial protection.
- **Carpet**: While more forgiving than hardwood, new carpet should still be protected in high-traffic paths. Use plastic carpet protector or clean blankets.
- **Tile**: Can chip or crack under heavy furniture. Use protective covering and rubber-wheeled dollies only.
- **LVP/Laminate**: Can scratch or dent. Protect similarly to hardwood.

**Wall Protection**: New paint and drywall are vulnerable:
- Use corner guards on all turns and doorways where furniture will pass
- Apply protective padding to door frames and wall edges
- Move blankets hung on walls provide protection in narrow hallways
- Don't lean furniture or boxes against walls

**Door Protection**: Remove doors that might be hit during moving if practical, or pad door edges and use door stoppers to prevent wall damage.

**Handrails and Fixtures**: Wrap handrails, light fixtures, and other protruding elements that could be hit by furniture or boxes.

## What's Not Included in New Construction

Understand what you'll need to provide or install yourself:

**Window Treatments**: Builders rarely include window treatments. You'll need:
- Blinds or shades for privacy (especially important in Denver's sunny climate)
- Measurements for all windows
- Installation (DIY or professional)
- Temporary coverage for moving-in period (bedsheet clips or temporary paper shades)

Consider measuring windows before closing so you can order treatments immediately. For large or custom windows common in new construction, this could take several weeks for delivery.

**Landscaping**: New construction landscaping is typically minimal:
- Front yards often get basic landscaping (sod or hydroseed and a few shrubs)
- Backyards frequently get just rough grading and possibly sod or hydroseed
- No plants, gardens, fencing, or decorative landscaping usually included
- Irrigation systems may or may not be included (check your contract)
- Expect to invest significantly in completing landscaping
- Denver's HOAs often have landscaping completion deadlines (commonly within the first year)

**Fencing**: Most new Denver homes don't include fencing. You'll need to:
- Check HOA requirements for fence style, height, and materials
- Get bids from fencing contractors
- Possibly coordinate with neighbors for shared fence lines
- Budget $2,000-$8,000+ depending on yard size and materials

**Garage Shelving and Storage**: Garages typically come empty. Consider:
- Garage shelving systems
- Overhead storage racks
- Workbenches
- Tool organization

**Closet Organizers**: While you'll have basic closet rods and shelves, many homeowners upgrade to:
- Custom closet systems
- Additional shelving and organization
- Shoe racks and specialty storage
Denver has several closet system providers, or DIY kits are available at home improvement stores.

**Additional Outlets or Fixtures**: If you want outlets or light fixtures beyond what the builder included, you'll need an electrician after move-in. Common additions:
- Additional garage outlets
- USB outlet upgrades
- Accent lighting
- Ceiling fans in rooms where they weren't included

**Mailbox Installation**: In some developments, individual mailbox installation is homeowner responsibility. Check with your builder and HOA.

**House Numbers**: Some builders include house numbers, others don't. This is often on your punch list.

**Deck or Patio Furniture**: Obviously your responsibility, but plan for:
- Furniture suitable for Denver's weather
- Umbrellas or shade structures (Denver's sun is intense)
- Outdoor rugs and accessories
- Grills and outdoor cooking equipment

## Homeowners Association (HOA) Considerations

Many new Denver developments have HOAs with specific rules:

**HOA Documents**: You'll receive HOA documents before closing. Actually read them. Pay attention to:
- Architectural control requirements (what changes require approval)
- Landscaping requirements and deadlines
- Exterior maintenance rules
- Parking and vehicle restrictions
- Pet policies
- Rental restrictions if you might ever rent the property
- Fee amounts and what they cover

**Move-In Requirements**: Some HOAs have move-in rules:
- Required move-in registration or notification
- Restrictions on moving times/days
- Elevator reservations in multi-family buildings
- Specific entrance requirements for moving trucks
- Deposit requirements for potential damage (refundable)

**Moving Truck Access**: In some developments:
- Large moving trucks may not be allowed on certain streets
- You may need to shuttle items from a staging area
- Specific parking areas may be designated for moving vehicles
- Narrow streets or construction may limit truck positioning

Discuss these considerations with your [moving company](/services) during planning. Professional movers experienced with Denver developments will be familiar with common access limitations and HOA requirements.

**Community Rules**: Understand community standards:
- Garbage can placement and visibility rules
- Lawn maintenance expectations
- Holiday decoration limitations
- Vehicle parking (no boats, RVs, commercial vehicles in some communities)

**Amenity Access**: Your HOA fees provide amenities. Get information on:
- Clubhouse reservations
- Pool access and guest policies
- Fitness center hours
- Parks and playground locations
- Walking trails and common areas

## Dealing with Construction Zones

Moving into a developing neighborhood has unique challenges:

**Ongoing Construction**: Your street might have multiple homes still under construction:
- Construction noise (often starts early morning)
- Construction traffic and workers
- Dust and debris
- Blocked streets or driveways occasionally
- Limited street parking due to construction vehicles

**Road Conditions**: Many new developments have:
- Unpaved or partially paved roads
- Construction mud or dust depending on weather
- Missing curbs or sidewalks
- Temporary road surfaces before final paving
This can affect moving truck access. Discuss with your movers and possibly schedule moving for dry weather to avoid muddy conditions.

**Utility Work**: Even after your home is done, utility installation for other phases might continue:
- Gas line trenching
- Internet/cable installation
- Water/sewer work
- Electric line burial

This can cause temporary utility disruptions, street closures, or muddy conditions.

**Temporary Inconveniences**: New neighborhoods often lack:
- Mature landscaping and shade
- Established community feel
- Nearby retail or services (common in edge developments)
- Complete amenities (pools or clubhouses might still be under construction)

**Debris and Cleanup**: New construction generates debris:
- Nail sweeps are critical (some builders provide, others don't)
- Request thorough post-construction cleaning before move-in
- Check yards and driveways for construction debris
- Keep an eye out for nails in the first months—they're hazardous to tires

**Security Considerations**: Construction zones attract:
- Theft of construction materials (sometimes from occupied homes too)
- Workers and subcontractors who don't necessarily belong in your home
- Lock doors and garages even during the day
- Consider security system activation immediately
- Get to know neighbors quickly for community awareness

## Timing Considerations Specific to Denver

Colorado's climate affects new construction timing:

**Winter Construction**: Denver construction continues year-round, but winter brings challenges:
- Concrete work is temperature-dependent (can't pour in freezing temps)
- Some exterior work pauses during extreme cold or snow
- Delays are more common November-March
- If your projected completion is winter, expect potential delays

**Spring Rush**: Spring is Denver's busiest:
- Many homeowners try to close and move during nice weather
- Construction ramps up after winter
- Moving companies are busiest April-June
- Book movers well in advance for spring moves

**Summer Advantages**: Summer is ideal for new construction completion:
- Fastest construction progress
- Best weather for moving
- Good time for exterior work and landscaping
- Easiest for settling in before school starts if you have kids

**Fall Considerations**: Fall is excellent for moving:
- Weather is still good
- Less busy than spring/summer
- Holiday deadlines motivate builders
- But winter weather starts by October/November in Denver

## Special Considerations for Different Property Types

New construction varies by property type:

### Single-Family Homes in Developments

**Builder Reputation**: Research your builder:
- Check with Colorado Better Business Bureau
- Read online reviews
- Talk to other homeowners in the development
- Ask for references from the builder

**Development Phase**: Where your home falls in the development affects experience:
- Early phase: You're a pioneer; limited neighbors, but also more construction impacts
- Middle phase: Good balance of established feel and selection
- Final phase: Most amenities done, established community, but you're dealing with last construction

**Utility Setup**: Coordinate with neighbors on services like snow removal or lawn care if you want to share costs.

### Townhomes and Condos

**Attached Living**: Consider:
- Shared wall soundproofing (ask about insulation during construction)
- HOA is more involved in your property
- Exterior maintenance is usually HOA responsibility
- Move-in coordination might be tighter (protect common areas, elevator scheduling)

**Parking**: Verify your parking:
- Number of assigned spaces
- Guest parking locations
- Garage vs. carport vs. open spaces

### Semi-Custom and Custom Homes

**More Complexity**: Custom homes involve:
- More decisions throughout construction
- Potentially longer timelines
- More opportunities for delays
- More expensive upgrades and changes

**Higher Stakes**: Custom homes are larger investments:
- More thorough inspections recommended
- Consider hiring an independent inspector throughout construction
- Document everything extensively
- Ensure all custom features work correctly

## Financial Considerations

New construction involves specific financial elements:

**Final Walkthrough Costs**: After closing, you might discover:
- Additional items you want to upgrade immediately (light fixtures, fans, etc.)
- Window treatments needed right away
- Landscaping costs
- Fence installation costs
Budget for these immediate expenses beyond your down payment and closing costs.

**Utility Deposits**: First-time service initiation sometimes requires deposits:
- Electric/gas deposits (often waived with good credit)
- Water deposits (common in some municipalities)
- HOA setup fees

**Immediate Maintenance**: Even new homes need:
- HVAC filters (establish regular changing schedule)
- Lawn/landscaping maintenance (sprinkler programming, fertilization, etc.)
- Gutter cleaning (especially if built near trees)

**Property Taxes**: New construction tax assessments:
- Initial assessed value may be estimated
- Reassessment will occur after completion
- Budget for potential tax increases after first year
- New Denver-area developments sometimes have metro districts or special assessments adding to property tax burden

## Creating Your Move-In Timeline

Plan your new construction move with this timeline:

**3 Months Before Projected Completion**:
- Confirm construction status with weekly builder updates
- Start planning move logistics
- Research and book moving companies
- Begin measuring for window treatments
- Decide on immediate purchases (furniture, decor, etc.)

**6-8 Weeks Before**:
- Conduct pre-drywall walkthrough if timing works
- Start utility setup process
- Order window treatments
- Research landscaping and fencing contractors
- Plan flooring protection strategy

**3-4 Weeks Before**:
- Confirm completion timeline with builder
- Conduct final walkthrough
- Create detailed punch list
- Get [moving estimate](/get-a-quote) updated based on actual move date
- Begin packing non-essentials
- Schedule internet/cable installation

**1-2 Weeks Before**:
- Verify Certificate of Occupancy issued
- Final builder walkthrough
- Confirm all punch list items completed or scheduled
- Verify utility activation dates
- Confirm moving company details
- Pack in earnest

**Closing Day**:
- Final walkthrough (make sure no new damage)
- Close on the home
- Get all keys, garage remotes, and access codes
- Get all warranty documents, appliance manuals, and builder contact info
- Visit the home and measure for any last-minute needs

**Day Before Move**:
- Deep clean new home if desired (easier before furniture)
- Install floor protection
- Set up utilities
- Program garage door openers
- Make sure home is completely ready

**Move-In Day**:
- Arrive before movers
- Walk through protection measures
- Direct furniture placement
- Do final inspection after movers leave
- Take photos of home's condition after move-in

**First Week**:
- Unpack essentials
- Test all systems and appliances thoroughly
- Note any issues for warranty claims
- Meet neighbors
- Enjoy your new home!

## Common New Construction Pitfalls to Avoid

Learn from common mistakes:

**Rushing Closing**: Don't let builder pressure rush closing if issues remain. Your leverage evaporates after closing. Insist on completion of punch list items or get credits for incomplete work.

**Skipping Professional Inspection**: Even new homes can have issues. Professional inspectors catch problems homeowners miss. The $300-500 cost is worth the protection.

**Inadequate Protection During Move**: New homes show damage easily. Over-protect rather than under-protect. The cost of protection is tiny compared to refinishing scratched floors.

**Poor Documentation**: Take extensive photos and videos before closing and after moving. Document every issue. You'll need this for warranty claims.

**Not Understanding HOA**: Violating HOA rules, even unknowingly, can result in fines. Read documents thoroughly and ask questions.

**Waiting Too Long for Utilities**: Set up utilities early. Some providers have delays for new addresses.

**Unrealistic Landscaping Expectations**: Budget and timeline for landscaping completion. Many HOAs give one year but it's best not to wait that long. Denver's growing season is limited; don't waste a season.

**Ignoring Warranty Timelines**: Report issues promptly. Don't wait until warranty expires. Keep meticulous records of all communications.

**Underestimating Settling**: New homes settle. Minor cracks, sticking doors, or drywall imperfections in the first year are normal. Document them for 11-month walkthrough but don't panic about minor settling.

**Not Planning for Unfinished Elements**: Budget for window treatments, landscaping, fencing, and other items builders don't include. These costs add up quickly—easily $5,000-15,000+ for a typical home.
